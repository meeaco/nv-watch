### [Dense Fluorination of Nanodiamonds for Quantum Sensing](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.78782)
- Michal Gulka et al. (13 authors) · Advanced Science · created 2026-11-27 · published 2026-11-26 · via Crossref · relevance score 8
- Abstract: ABSTRACT Shallow nitrogen‐vacancy (NV) centers in high‐pressure high‐temperature nanodiamonds (HPHT NDs) enable nanoscale quantum sensing and bioimaging, but their charge and spin stabilities are dominated by surface chemistry. Fluorine termination of diamond is of interest because of its chemical inertness, suppression of non‐specific molecular interactions, and beneficial electronic effects of NV centers. Here we establish a scalable fluorination route for HPHT NDs based on high‐pressure treatment with elemental F 2 . We show that pre‐hydrogenation of ∼28 nm HPHT NDs enables dense C─F surface termination, resulting in markedly improved NV performance. Spin‐lattice relaxation times ( T 1 ) are extended. Electron paramagnetic resonance spectroscopy reveals a ∼3‐fold increase in the concentration of negatively‐charged NV centers, accompanied by a 2.3‐fold increase in single‐particle brightness. Spectral deconvolution confirms unprecedented NV − charge stability with NV − fraction increasing to ∼94% upon fluorination compared ∼57% for tri‐acid oxidized NDs. Finally, taking advantage of colloidal stability of fluorinated NDs in water, we demonstrate adsorption‐free, concentration‐dependent detection of gadolinium (Gd 3+ ) in chelated electroneutral form (gadoteridol) using in‐solution T 1 relaxometry. These results establish fluorination as an effective strategy to stabilize shallow NV centers and expand the utility of NDs for quantitative sensing in aqueous environments.
