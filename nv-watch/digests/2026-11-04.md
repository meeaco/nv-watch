### [Adaptive Self-Optimization of Polarization Efficiency for Low-Power NV Center Thermometers Based on PAS-Q](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04588)
- Yifan Wang, Li Xing, Ruigang Wang, Zheng Qian · arXiv:2611.04588 · quant-ph (cats: quant-ph) · submitted 2026-11-03 · relevance score 10 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond have attracted considerable attention for micro- and nanoscale thermometry owing to their high precision and spatial resolution. However, achieving sufficient electron spin polarization and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) typically requires high-power or long-duration laser excitation, which introduces heating and degrades measurement accuracy and stability. To address this trade-off, a reinforcement learning (RL)-based approach is proposed to optimize spin polarization under low-heating constraints. First, a physical model integrating five-level optical pumping rates and NV spin polarization dynamics is established. A temperature-rise constraint model is then developed based on pulsed optically detected magnetic resonance (P-ODMR) measurements under varying laser powers and pulse widths in an external magnetic field. Subsequently, a Progressive Action-Space Q-learning (PAS-Q) algorithm is employed to explore the parameter space and identify optimal laser power and pulse-width combinations. The agent is trained through numerical simulations and the resulting strategy is implemented in a hardware system. Experimental results demonstrate a 44% reduction in laser-induced heating, a 33% increase in spin polarization rate based on T1 relaxation analysis, and a 71% reduction in optimization time compared with traditional methods. This approach enables efficient spin polarization with reduced laser heating and improved optimization efficiency, and may be extended to other heat-sensitive quantum spin sensors, including silicon-vacancy centers in diamond and divacancy defects in silicon carbide.

### [The Impact of Internal Electric Fields on Accurate Diamond Magnetometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03984)
- Jack Faller et al. (5 authors) · arXiv:2611.03984 · quant-ph (cats: quant-ph) · submitted 2026-11-02 · relevance score 3 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Nitrogen-vacancy (NV) center spin ensembles are a promising platform for accurate vector magnetometry, as they offer measurements referenced to a spin Hamiltonian in an anisotropic crystal with native vector sensing capabilities. However, charged dopants within the diamond produce random internal electric fields (RIEFs) that shift NV spin-transition frequencies, and their impact on the accuracy of NV magnetometry has not been investigated. Here, we characterize RIEFs' effect on NV spin-transition spectra and propose an analytical fitting function to improve accuracy in their presence. We build a Monte Carlo RIEF model that reproduces the asymmetric lineshapes observed in experimental optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) spectra, and use it as a testbed to quantify RIEF-induced errors by simulating ODMR spectra including RIEFs and comparing reconstructed magnetic fields to the known model input. With standard analysis approaches, we observe systematic errors exceeding the g-factor uncertainty in fields below $\sim$ 5 mT, while the proposed fitting function reduces the error by nearly an order of magnitude. Our results demonstrate that RIEFs can play a significant role in the systematic error budget for NV magnetometry, and offer a path towards improved accuracy in NV quantum sensing.

### [Class-Selective Spin Activity of Two Spectrally Distinct Color Centers in Oxygen-Annealed Hexagonal Boron Nitride](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsphotonics.6c01777)
- Yuxing Liu et al. (6 authors) · ACS Photonics · created 2026-11-03 · published 2026-11-02 · via OpenAlex · relevance score 10
- Abstract: Abstract Hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) hosts a variety of room-temperature single-photon emitters, among which optically addressable spin defects are attractive for quantum sensing and spin–photon interfaces. While the negatively charged boron vacancy is well established as an ensemble spin system, bright and individually addressable spin-active emitters remain scarce, and it is unknown whether spin activity is a property of an entire optical emitter class or only of a subset. We have recently shown that oxygen annealing of hBN activates two spectrally distinct emitter populations with so-far unresolved spin properties. Here, we perform optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) screening of 158 individual emitters across both populations. We find that spin activity is carried exclusively by the Type I population: 9 of 100 Type I emitters display ODMR with coexisting S = 1 and S = 1/2 transitions and zero-field splitting (ZFS) parameters of D ≈ 977 MHz and E ≈ 174 MHz, while all 58 Type II emitters remain spin-silent. Pulsed ODMR on all three transition branches reveals spin–lattice relaxation times up to 19.1 μs and spin echo coherence times up to 56.0 ns, with the S = 1/2 branch showing systematically shorter T2 than the two S = 1 branches in every spin-active emitter. The exclusive correlation of spin activity with one optical class and the branch-resolved coherence asymmetry provide experimental constraints for identifying the microscopic origin of carbon- and oxygen-related color centers in hBN.

### [Experimental investigation of using diamond crystal as a high temperature sensor](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rinma.2026.101075)

- Abstract: This paper investigates the feasibility of using diamond in high-temperature environments. These miniaturized sensors made from materials like 3C-SiC, diamond, and graphite exploit the expansion of the crystal lattice due to neutron irradiation, which is then reduced upon heating. Measuring lattice parameters before and after heating allows for accurate temperature determination within a few degrees up to 1000 °C. The study focused on a diamond sensor, calculating fast neutron flux and activity in the Tehran research reactor core. Results showed a significant reduction in diamond activity after approximately 3 months. Pre-and post-irradiation analyses using X-ray fluorescence for impurities and X-ray diffraction (XRD) for phase identification and crystal characteristics were performed. ### [High-cooperativity coupling and spin-resolved extinction of tin-vacancy centers in a diamond-like microcavity](https://doi.org/10.1103/jhhs-kls1)
- Kerim Köster et al. (19 authors) · PRX Quantum · created 2026-08-07 · published 2026-11-02 · via OpenAlex · relevance score 5
- Abstract: The tin-vacancy (SnV) center in diamond is a promising spin-photon interface for quantum networks, combining favorable optical properties with spin coherence above 1K. Unfolding the full potential requires cavity enhancement to increase photon-emitter coupling efficiency. Here, we demonstrate cavity-enhanced light-matter coupling of SnV centers in a fully tunable Fabry-Pérot microcavity operating at temperatures down to 1K with in-situ magnetic field control. We access the diamond-like regime of hybrid cavity modes through integration of low-roughness diamond membranes, where the field is concentrated inside the diamond and Purcell enhancement is maximized. Diamond-like modes deliver a more than two-fold increase in the effective Purcell factor over air-like modes, reaching $C_0 = 4.1(1)$ compared to $C_0 = 1.85(5)$ in the air-like case, while simultaneously relaxing mechanical stability requirements. Resonant probing reveals coherent cavity-emitter coupling with 96% extinction contrast and a coherent cooperativity of $C = 4.0(14)$. By applying a magnetic field, we further achieve spin-resolved cavity extinction, observing spin-selective optical transitions with a contrast of ${\cal C}_{\rm spin} = 0.91$. These results establish SnV centers in diamond coupled to open Fabry-Pérot microcavities as a promising platform for efficient spin-photon interfaces.

### [Experimental Comparison of Filter Integration Strategies for Nitrogen Vacancy Center Photoluminescence Detection in Optically Detected Magnetic Resonance Measurements](https://doi.org/10.1002/pssa.70583)
- Kai Kühnlenz, Mario Bähr, Christian Möller, Thomas Ortlepp · physica status solidi (a) · created 2026-11-04 · published 2026-11-3 · via Crossref · relevance score 3
- Abstract: Optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) based on nitrogen‐vacancy (NV) centers in diamond is a powerful technique for sensing applications where the efficient detection of NV photoluminescence is a critical performance factor. Although optical filters are routinely employed to suppress the green excitation light, their integration at the detector level is often treated as a secondary design aspect. In this work, we present a systematic experimental comparison of different filter integration strategies applied directly to otherwise identical photodiodes and evaluate their impact on ODMR performance. The study was conducted using a breadboard ODMR setup comprising a green laser excitation source, a high‐pressure high‐temperature (HPHT) diamond containing NV centers with a concentration in the ppm range, microwave excitation, and lock‐in‐based signal detection. The filter variants were characterized using both ODMR contrast measurements at the NV − zero‐phonon line and optical density (OD) values extracted from laser power versus photocurrent measurements. Particular emphasis is placed on, showing that a higher OD does not always correlate with a higher ODMR contrast. We suggest that thin‐film filter approaches offer a favorable compromise between excitation suppression and ODMR signal quality, providing useful design guidance for NV‐center‐based sensing applications.
