### [Simulation of Spin–Dependent Quantum Magnetometry for Weak Geomagnetic Field Detection Using Optically Detected Magnetic Resonance](https://doi.org/10.69626/mse.2026.0120)
- Gul Faroz Ahmad, Hilal Ahmad, Samiya Shah, C. A. Duque · MatSci Express · created 2026-10-23 · published 2026-7-1 · via Crossref · relevance score 5
- Abstract: Detecting weak magnetic fields is important for geophysics, navigation, space weather monitoring, and quantum sensing. This study presents a simulation-based framework for a spin-dependent quantum magnetometer operating within the geomagnetic field range. The model represents an effective two-level spin defect and relates Zeeman splitting to optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR). A Lorentzian resonance profile incorporates spin coherence decay, linewidth broadening, and Gaussian noise. Simulations across magnetic fields of 25–65 µT, using nitrogen-vacancy center-like parameters, demonstrate a linear relationship between resonance frequency and magnetic field. Longer transverse relaxation times produce narrower resonances and improve sensitivity, whereas noise reduces the signal-to-noise ratio. Nevertheless, the resonance remains resolvable under the investigated conditions. A noise-to-slope estimation method predicts room-temperature magnetic sensitivity in the nanotesla range, consistent in order of magnitude with reported solid-state quantum magnetometers. These findings highlight the potential of spin-based quantum magnetometry for geomagnetic sensing and provide a computational foundation for optimizing coherence, linewidth, and noise before device fabrication.
