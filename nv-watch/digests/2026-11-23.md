### [Correlating Spin and Optical Properties of Quantum Emitters in hBN](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsnano.6c14413)
- Nika Teran et al. (7 authors) · ACS Nano · created 2026-08-06 · published 2026-11-21 · via OpenAlex · relevance score 6
- Abstract: Optically addressable spin defects in hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) are well suited for near-surface quantum sensing. They offer bright, wavelength-tunable single-photon emission with high optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) contrast at room temperature. Here, we synthesize a high density of visible defects in carbon-doped hBN flakes with a high yield (14%) of ODMR-active emitters. We find that the zero-field splitting parameter D is directly correlated with the zero-phonon line of an emitter, while the ODMR contrast shows no such correlation. We further analyze an individual narrowband defect showing 68% ODMR contrast and enhance its photon collection by ∼40% using a solid immersion lens. By advancing both the practical synthesis of the spin complexes and the understanding of their microscopic origin, our results move us toward the deterministic creation of ODMR-active quantum emitters.

### [Diamond and related carbon-based materials for biomedical devices: processing, surface engineering, and functional interfaces](https://doi.org/10.1080/26941112.2026.2763204)
- Lin Jiang, Lijuan Zheng, Chengyong Wang · Functional Diamond · created 2026-11-22 · published 2026-11-20 · via OpenAlex · relevance score 6
- Abstract: Diamond and related carbon-based materials are being explored for biomedical devices that require durable contact surfaces, stable electrochemical transduction, or functional optical and biological interfaces. This Review examines crystalline diamond, nanodiamond, nanocrystalline and ultrananocrystalline diamond, boron-doped and fluorescent diamond, diamond-like carbon (DLC), and diamond-containing composites. We discuss how HPHT synthesis, CVD/MPCVD growth, doping, defect engineering, and interfacial modification connect processing with structure, surface behavior, and device function. Representative studies in surgical and dental instruments, orthopedic and cardiovascular technologies, ophthalmology, drug delivery, bioimaging, biosensing, neural interfaces, and regenerative medicine are compared in terms of their methods, reported outcomes, and limitations. Across these areas, we identify opportunities in wear-resistant coatings, conductive diamond electrodes, and functionalized nanodiamond carriers and probes for drug delivery and bioimaging. Surface chemistry, interfacial adhesion, and device architecture emerge as recurring factors governing mechanical durability, electrochemical performance, optical readout, and biological interactions. Particular attention is given to coating failure, wear debris, corrosion, material-dependent biological responses, fouling, signal drift, and device integration. We distinguish direct biomedical findings from manufacturing studies that provide useful but unvalidated engineering principles. Finally, we discuss application-specific comparison metrics, relevant medical-device testing frameworks, and challenges in translating diamond functions into reproducible, safe, and clinically useful technologies.

### [Transparent conductive electrodes for electrical spin readout in solid-state systems](https://doi.org/10.1103/4y9n-4kww)
- D. Reinhardt, A. Witte, P. Schlupp, R. Wunderlich · Physical Review Applied · created 2026-11-21 · published 2026-11-20 · via OpenAlex · relevance score 5
- Abstract: In this work, we demonstrate a transparent electrode structure on a diamond surface for spatial defect mapping and the electrical spin state readout of defect ensembles. For this purpose, a 210 nm thin bar-shaped layer of tin doped indium oxide (ITO) is directly deposited on the diamond surface. The layer is both transparent in the optical wavelength range and electrically conductive and allows therefore simultaneous acquisition of photocurrent (PC) and photoluminescence (PL) near the ITO/diamond interface. The spin system considered are nitrogen vacancy (NV) center ensembles intrinsically present in the diamond sample. The targeted spin manipulation of the NV centers is achieved by a CW microwave field applied via the near field of a thin copper wire. The sample is mounted directly on a printed circuit board (PCB) that houses all necessary electronics for the low noise acquisition of the PC. Simultaneous detection of conventional PL by confocal fluorescence microscopy enables a direct comparison with optical readout techniques. Measurements of different 2D areas parallel as well as perpendicular to the ITO/diamond interface at different bias voltages are presented. Furthermore, we performed photoelectric detection of magnetic resonance (PDMR) studies and compared the results with optical detection of magnetic resonance (ODMR) measurements. Our findings demonstrate that an ITO layer can be reliably produced on a diamond surface and effectively utilized as a transparent electrode, enabling robust PC detection over areas of several hundred square micrometers. This opens new avenues for optimized electrical readout techniques for defect based quantum sensing in solids.
