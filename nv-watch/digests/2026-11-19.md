### [Multiplexed quantum sensing reveals coordinated cellular thermomagnetic regulation by mitochondria](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpblue.2026.100166)
- Md Shakil Bin Kashem et al. (14 authors) · Cell Press Blue · created 2025-10-10 · published 2026-11-01 · via OpenAlex · relevance score 6
- Abstract: Mitochondria are multifunctional organelles that convert the potential energy stored in nutrients and intermediary metabolites into both heat and an electrochemical proton-motive force. However, how these outputs are synchronized in cells remains an enduring question. In this work, leveraging multiplexed nanodiamond quantum sensors to monitor both changes in temperature and magnetic field fluctuations in single primary cells obtained from diverse tissues in adult mice, we identified thermomagnetic correlation profiles uncovering a regulatory feedback loop in which the cell draws upon available intracellular iron to maintain the mitochondrial electrochemical gradient. These profiles reverse in cells derived from a mouse model of Leigh syndrome and raise the intriguing possibility that primary mitochondrial diseases can be understood as disorders of thermomagnetic homeostasis.

### [Methods for traceable scanning magnetometry using single nitrogen–vacancy centers in diamond: determining orientation, distance, and localization](https://doi.org/10.3389/frqst.2026.1940172)
- Nikhita Khera et al. (10 authors) · Frontiers in Quantum Science and Technology · created 2026-11-19 · published 2026-11-19 · via Crossref · relevance score 4
- Abstract: Individual scannable nitrogen–vacancy (NV) centers in single crystal diamond nanostructures enable the nanoscale, quantitative imaging of magnetic stray fields. Nevertheless, important parameters such as distance between the NV center and the sample and the orientation of the NV high symmetry axis are often not known precisely and enter data evaluation as free-fitting parameters. We here use scanning NV imaging on micro-patterned, perpendicularly magnetized stripes and discs. From these measurements, we directly infer NV–sample distance d NV and the NV’s azimuthal orientation without the need of external vector magnet control. We determine d NV = 31.5 nm, while inferring the azimuthal orientation with a precision of 3°. We additionally employ commercially available silicon needles to image the apex topography of our diamond nanostructures to detect surface contamination. Simultaneously, monitoring NV fluorescence as a function of the needle’s position allows us to estimate the lateral placement of the NV inside the diamond nanostructure.

### [Effect of deuteration in fluorescence- and phosphorescence-resolved optically detected magnetic resonance of metal-free dual singlet-triplet emitting OLEDs](https://doi.org/10.1103/sgwz-sr72)
- Frank D. Braun et al. (7 authors) · Physical Review Materials · created 2026-11-18 · published 2026-11-17 · via OpenAlex · relevance score 3
- Abstract: Dual singlet-triplet-emitting organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs) based on metal-free room-temperature phosphorescence (RTP) materials offer unique opportunities to study electronic and nuclear spin dynamics. We investigate the influence of nuclear hyperfine fields, i.e., deuteration, on spin-dependent exciton formation in simple guest:host OLEDs based on the RTP compound 11,12-dimethyldibenzo[a,c]phenazine (DMDB) embedded in a 4,4\ensuremath{'}-bis(N-carbazolyl)-1,1\ensuremath{'}-biphenyl (CBP) matrix by using fluorescence- and phosphorescence-resolved optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR). The ODMR response is governed by the interplay of the polaron-pair (PP) mechanism, where electronic spin precession in hyperfine fields controls exciton generation, and the triplet-exciton polaron (TEP) interaction, which mediates spin-dependent quenching of triplet excitons by polarons and hence phosphorescence. By combining phase-sensitive lock-in detection with a density-matrix-based description of the spin dynamics, we succeed in disentangling the strongly convoluted resonance line shapes arising from the superposition of the two processes. Simultaneous analysis of in-phase and quadrature lock-in signals yields consistent hyperfine-field strengths and electron-hole and exciton recombination rates. In contrast to pure hydrocarbon materials, deuteration reduces hyperfine-field strengths by only approximately 20%, which we attribute to the dominant contribution of the nitrogen nuclear spin to hyperfine interactions. At the same time, the decay rate of the DMDB triplet excited state is increased by almost a factor of two, in agreement with quantum-chemical calculations. The results demonstrate how the effects of deuteration can be extracted from ODMR spectra despite the strong influence of overlapping PP and TEP contributions.
