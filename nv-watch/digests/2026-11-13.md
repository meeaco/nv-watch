### [Optically-detected room-temperature spin-coherence from the GaV$^-$ in diamond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16281)
- Adam D. Dodson et al. (9 authors) · arXiv:2611.16281 · cond-mat.mtrl-sci (cats: cond-mat.mtrl-sci, quant-ph) · submitted 2026-11-12 · relevance score 5 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Solid-state quantum defects (point crystal imperfections with optically addressable spins) are among the most promising platforms for quantum information science due to their long-lived electron and nuclear spin lifetimes. The negatively-charged nitrogen-vacancy (NV$^-$) and negatively-charged group IV vacancies (G4V's) in diamond are some the most promising quantum defects but, the NV$^-$ is highly susceptible to charge noise and has low emission from its zero-phonon line while the G4V's require low operating temperatures. Here we report the physical properties of the newly discovered negatively-charged gallium-vacancy in diamond, GaV$^-$. We find that the GaV$^-$, created through FIB implantation and high-pressure high-temperature annealing, exhibits a combination of the best properties of both the NV$^-$ and G4V's: optically-detectable spin coherence at room temperature ($T_2(293K) = 1.06 \mu$s), a large Debye-Waller factor $\sim$0.4, a short radiative lifetime ($\sim$3.83 ns), strong temperature dependence of the zero-field splitting (16x that of the NV$^-$), and charge-noise-protected D$_{3d}$ symmetry. These features (along with improved performance at low temperature) position the newly discovered GaV$^-$ as a key resource for quantum sensing, networking, and computation.

### [Detrital Remanent Magnetization Analysis at the Particle Scale: Implications for Prebiotic Origins of Homochirality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14905)
- S. Furkan Ozturk et al. (8 authors) · arXiv:2611.14905 · physics.chem-ph (cats: physics.chem-ph) · submitted 2026-11-11 · relevance score 4 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Detrital remanent magnetization (DRM), the alignment of ferromagnetic grains with the geomagnetic field during sedimentation, is the primary mechanism of paleomagnetic remanence acquisition in sedimentary rocks, yet its microphysical mechanism remains contested. Measured DRM intensities consistently fall below theoretical predictions-the long-standing "DRM efficiency gap"-and no single-particle resolving observations have been available to test DRM acquisition models directly. Here we apply quantum diamond microscopy (QDM) to laboratory-redeposited lacustrine sediments to obtain the first single-particle magnetizations of DRM carriers. We compare these measurements with the results of bulk magnetometry and a Langevin-based kinetic model. We observe a statistically significant mean alignment of individual detrital particle moments with the applied field, $10\%$ when averaged as unit vectors and $24\%$ when weighted by moment magnitude, with the vector sum of all particles reproducing the direction of the bulk DRM, which is $\sim1.8\%$ of saturation remanence. Particle-level analysis shows that alignment rises with particle moment, a trend reproduced by our kinetic model in which floc-hosted magnetite rotates toward the field until the surrounding sediment locks it in place. Even where post-depositional disturbance is minimal and the measured DRM reaches the upper end of reported efficiencies, it remains well below its theoretical maximum, limited in comparable measure by incomplete particle-level alignment and sub-saturation within-particle magnetization. Beyond its implications for the DRM mechanism, this strong particle-level bias provides a quantitative foundation for magnetic minerals as natural chiral agents, a proposed pathway for the emergence of biomolecular homochirality on the prebiotic Earth.

### [Zero-Field Optical Readout of Quintet States in Through-Space Coupled Tetracene Oligomers at Room Temperature](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15713)
- Jeannine Gr\"une et al. (5 authors) · arXiv:2611.15713 · cond-mat.mtrl-sci (cats: cond-mat.mtrl-sci, physics.chem-ph) · submitted 2026-11-11 · relevance score 3 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Optically addressable electron spins are promising platforms for quantum technologies, with molecular systems offering chemical control over their spin and optical properties. Achieving optical readout of molecular spin states at room temperature and without an applied magnetic field is an important step towards their practical implementation. Quintet states generated via singlet fission are promising optically addressable multilevel qudits, but their intrinsic spin transitions at zero and weak magnetic fields remain largely unexplored, while substantial room-temperature quintet coherence has predominantly been demonstrated in structurally constrained molecular architectures. Here, we combine zero-field optically detected magnetic resonance (zero-field ODMR) with transient and pulsed electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) to investigate the magnetic-field dependence and coherent spin properties of quintet states in conformationally flexible, through-space coupled tetracene oligomers. We demonstrate zero-field optical readout of quintet states at room temperature in both tetracene dimers and hexamers, and map the response of optically detected transitions to weak applied magnetic fields. Complementary EPR measurements support quintet formation through stochastic exchange fluctuations and demonstrate a room-temperature phase-memory time of 446 ns, showing that the conformational dynamics enabling high-spin formation remain compatible with substantial spin coherence. Together, these results integrate field-free optical readout, weak-field response and room-temperature spin coherence within a chemically modular, through-space coupled molecular architecture, expanding the design space for optically addressable molecular qudits and quantum sensors.
