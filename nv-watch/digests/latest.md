### [Signatures of Kitaev quantum spin liquid states in $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$ revealed by nitrogen-vacancy spin-noise spectroscopy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33085)
- Jitender Kumar et al. (13 authors) · arXiv:2611.33085 · cond-mat.str-el (cats: cond-mat.mes-hall, cond-mat.str-el) · submitted 2026-11-24 · relevance score 5 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Bond-dependent magnetic interactions, strong spin-orbit coupling, and quantum fluctuations in $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$ make it an archetypical candidate for exotic Kitaev quantum spin liquid (QSL) states. While electronic transport, neutron scattering, optical and tunneling spectroscopies were used to probe global QSL signatures in large scale $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$ samples, nanoscale evidence of local QSL behavior via a non-invasive experimental probe is still missing. Here, we use diamond nitrogen-vacancy (NV) spin-noise spectroscopy to probe QSL states in {\alpha}-RuCl3 nanoflakes with thicknesses ranging from 8 to 150 nm over a temperature range of 4.3-250 K. Our measurements reveal a pronounced decrease in the NV spin-lattice relaxation time ($T_{1}$) below ~100 K in proximity to the $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$ flakes, indicating enhanced GHz-frequency magnetic fluctuations. This temperature scale is consistent with the energy scale associated with Kitaev interactions (~8 meV) and the emergence of low-energy fractionalized excitations. Below 50 K, thicker (> 90 nm) flakes exhibit a distinct bi-exponential $T_{1}$ decay, unlike the single-exponential behavior observed for off-flake and thinner (8 nm) flake measurements, suggesting an increasingly heterogeneous magnetic noise environment. These results establish NV spin-noise spectroscopy as a local, non-invasive probe of low-energy spin dynamics in $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$ nanoflakes and open a route to testing predicted noise signatures of fractionalized excitations in atomically thin QSL candidates.

### [Scalable spin squeezing in power-law-interacting X X Z models with disorder](https://doi.org/10.1103/npj5-82pg)
- Samuel E. Begg et al. (5 authors) · Physical Review A · created 2026-01-17 · published 2026-11-24 · via OpenAlex · relevance score 0
- Abstract: While spin squeezing has been traditionally considered in all-to-all interacting models, recent works have shown that it can also occur in systems with power-law interactions, enabling direct tests in Rydberg atoms, trapped ions, ultracold atoms, and nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond. For the latter, Wu et al. Nature 646 (2025) demonstrated that spin squeezing is heavily affected by positional disorder, reducing any capacity for a practical squeezing advantage, which requires scalability with the system size. In this Letter we explore the robustness of spin squeezing in two-dimensional lattices with a fraction of unoccupied lattice sites. Using semiclassical modeling, we demonstrate the existence of scalable squeezing in power-law interacting XXZ models up to a disorder threshold, above which it breaks down. We produce a phase diagram for scalable squeezing, and demonstrate the impact of critical slowing down close to the phase boundary. Our work is consistent with—and offers a plausible explanation for—the absence of scalable squeezing in the aforementioned NV experiment, while illustrating the maximum disorder allowed for realizing scalable spin squeezing in a host of quantum simulators. We highlight a regime with substantial disorder tolerance and identify controlled defect creation as a promising route for scalable squeezing in solid-state systems.

### [Optimal Calibration of Qubit Detuning and Crosstalk](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2026-11-25-2284)
- David Shnaiderov et al. (6 authors) · Quantum · created 2026-11-25 · published 2026-11-25 · via Crossref · relevance score 0
- Abstract: Characterizing and calibrating physical qubits is essential for maintaining the performance of quantum processors. A key challenge in this process is crosstalk, which complicates the estimation of individual qubit detunings. In this work, we derive optimal strategies for estimating detuning and crosstalk parameters by optimizing Ramsey interference experiments using Fisher information and the Cramér–Rao bound. We compare several calibration protocols, including measurements of a single quadrature at multiple times and of two quadratures at a single time, for a fixed number of total measurements. Our results predict that the latter approach yields the highest precision and robustness in both cases of isolated and coupled qubits. We validate our approach experimentally using both a single NV center and superconducting transmon qubits. Our approach enables accurate parameter extraction with significantly fewer measurements, resulting in up to a 50% reduction in calibration time while maintaining estimation accuracy.
