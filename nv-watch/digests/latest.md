### [Nitrogen vacancy cluster-mediated ferroelectric domain growth in wurtzite AlN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31084)
- Zijian Zhou et al. (12 authors) · arXiv:2610.31084 · cond-mat.mtrl-sci (cats: cond-mat.mtrl-sci) · submitted 2026-10-22 · relevance score 2
- Abstract: Aluminum nitride-based ferroelectrics combine high remanent polarization, thermal stability, weak size effects, a wide bandgap and back-end process compatibility, making them promising for high density non-volatile memory. However, their high coercive field ($E_\mathrm{c}$) and unclear polarization switching mechanism limit further applications. Theoretical predictions indicate preferential reversed domain growth along the polar axis, whereas experiments show nearly isotropic growth or preferential growth perpendicular to this axis. Here, we combine density functional theory, Deep Potential molecular dynamics and experimental characterization to determine how nitrogen vacancies ($V_\mathrm{N}$) regulate domain growth and $E_\mathrm{c}$. In ideal crystal, strong lattice coupling along the polar axis drives preferential domain growth in this direction. $V_\mathrm{N}$ locally weakens this coupling and promotes polarization nucleation. The spatial distribution of $V_\mathrm{N}$ and the temporal sequence of vacancy induced switching jointly determine the direction and morphology of reversed domain growth. We further show that scandium doping promotes $V_\mathrm{N}$ formation, thereby enhancing defect mediated nucleation and reducing $E_\mathrm{c}$. These findings establish a multiscale mechanism governing thin-film domain-growth kinetics and macroscopic $E_\mathrm{c}$, reconciling experimental observations with ideal crystal simulations and providing defect engineering principles for low-voltage, reliable and scalable ferroelectric devices.

### [Helium-ion beam pattern imaging using irradiation-induced VB− centers in h-BN composite films](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0353236)
- Yiyuan Wu, Menghao Yue, Xiangyuan Li, Jijun Zou · Journal of Applied Physics · created 2026-10-22 · published 2026-10-22 · via Crossref · relevance score 6
- Abstract: Spatially resolved characterization of helium-ion irradiation is important for beam profiling, irradiation-process control, and radiation-effects research. In this work, irradiation-induced color centers in hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN) were investigated for helium-ion beam-pattern imaging. Spherical h-BN/cycloolefin copolymer (Sh-BN/COC) composite films were irradiated with 50 keV He+ ions, while h-BN crystalline particles irradiated with 1.5 MeV Xe+ ions were examined as a comparative high-damage system. The irradiation fluence ranged from 1×1013 to 1 × 1016 ions cm−2. Following irradiation, a broad near-infrared photoluminescence (PL) band centered at approximately 820 nm was observed under 532 nm laser excitation. Room-temperature optically detected magnetic resonance features distributed around 3.45 GHz further supported the attribution of this emission to negatively charged boron-vacancy (VB−) centers. The PL intensity exhibited a non-monotonic dependence on irradiation fluence, while severe structural disorder and PL quenching occurred at the highest fluence. For beam-pattern imaging, the Sh-BN/COC composite film was irradiated through a five-slit mask and subsequently characterized by two-dimensional PL mapping. The reconstructed PL image reproduced the mask-defined irradiation pattern and resolved adjacent features separated by 40 μm. Using the conventional half-pitch definition, this feature size corresponds to a nominal equivalent spatial frequency of 12.5 line pairs per millimeter (lp/mm). These results demonstrate the feasibility of using irradiation-induced VB− centers in h-BN composite films as passive spatial recorders for post-irradiation helium-ion beam-pattern imaging.

### [A van der Waals photon-ratchet for enhancing spin-defect emission and quantum sensing](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78802-4)
- E. Mayner et al. (9 authors) · Nature Communications · created 2026-10-22 · published 2026-10-22 · via Crossref · relevance score 4
- Abstract: Abstract Spin defects in two-dimensional materials are promising platforms for quantum information technologies and nanoscale sensing. The negatively charged boron vacancy ( $${\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{B}}^{-}$$ V B − ) in hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) is particularly attractive due to its sensitivity to temperature, magnetic fields, and pressure, but its application is limited by intrinsically weak photoluminescence (PL). Here we demonstrate a van der Waals heterostructure that enhances the PL intensity of $${\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{B}}^{-}$$ V B − by up to 45× at 4 Kelvin while preserving compatibility with ultrathin optoelectronic platforms. A quasi type-I band alignment at the PbI 2 –defected hBN interface facilitates efficient interlayer coupling to the defect, and strong spectral overlap supports fluorescence resonance energy transfer. Ab initio calculations indicate that this coupling enables a photon-ratcheting mechanism, in which optical absorption in PbI 2 generates a nonlinear polarization that drives $${\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{B}}^{-}$$ V B − excitation. This process is compatible with interlayer energy transfer while maintaining minimal electronic and spin hybridization and preserving optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) contrast. The heterostructure further exhibits improved sensitivity to external magnetic fields. These results establish a route for the enhancement of defect emission via interlayer optical coupling and introduce a general materials strategy for amplifying weak quantum defect signals in nanomaterials.
