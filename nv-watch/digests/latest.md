### [Signatures of Kitaev quantum spin liquid states in $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$ revealed by nitrogen-vacancy spin-noise spectroscopy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33085)
- Jitender Kumar et al. (13 authors) · arXiv:2611.33085 · cond-mat.str-el (cats: cond-mat.mes-hall, cond-mat.str-el) · submitted 2026-11-24 · relevance score 5 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Bond-dependent magnetic interactions, strong spin-orbit coupling, and quantum fluctuations in $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$ make it an archetypical candidate for exotic Kitaev quantum spin liquid (QSL) states. While electronic transport, neutron scattering, optical and tunneling spectroscopies were used to probe global QSL signatures in large scale $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$ samples, nanoscale evidence of local QSL behavior via a non-invasive experimental probe is still missing. Here, we use diamond nitrogen-vacancy (NV) spin-noise spectroscopy to probe QSL states in {\alpha}-RuCl3 nanoflakes with thicknesses ranging from 8 to 150 nm over a temperature range of 4.3-250 K. Our measurements reveal a pronounced decrease in the NV spin-lattice relaxation time ($T_{1}$) below ~100 K in proximity to the $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$ flakes, indicating enhanced GHz-frequency magnetic fluctuations. This temperature scale is consistent with the energy scale associated with Kitaev interactions (~8 meV) and the emergence of low-energy fractionalized excitations. Below 50 K, thicker (> 90 nm) flakes exhibit a distinct bi-exponential $T_{1}$ decay, unlike the single-exponential behavior observed for off-flake and thinner (8 nm) flake measurements, suggesting an increasingly heterogeneous magnetic noise environment. These results establish NV spin-noise spectroscopy as a local, non-invasive probe of low-energy spin dynamics in $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$ nanoflakes and open a route to testing predicted noise signatures of fractionalized excitations in atomically thin QSL candidates.

### [The Influence of Carbon Fibers and Cavitation-Synthesized Nanodiamonds on the Variatropic Concrete Characteristics](https://doi.org/10.3390/ma19234963)
- Evgenii M. Shcherban’ et al. (10 authors) · Materials · created 2026-11-24 · published 2026-11-23 · via OpenAlex · relevance score 3
- Abstract: Recent advances in construction materials science underscore the relevance and significant potential of using carbon-based additives to enhance the properties of cementitious composites. The current understanding of how these additives can be applied in the technology of variatropic (functionally graded) concretes remains limited. The goal of this study is to identify the best parameters for altering variatropic concretes made by centrifugation (C) and vibro-centrifugation (VC) with carbon-based additives (CF) and an aqueous nanodiamond suspension (ASN) to enhance their characteristics. An experimental investigation was conducted on the effects of combined modification with CF and ASN on the water absorption, density, compressive strength, and flexural strength of C and VC concretes. Optimal modification parameters were identified as 0.5% ASN and 0.8% CF. Under these conditions, the modified C and VC concretes reached peak compressive strengths of 51.2 MPa and 54.2 MPa, respectively—representing increases of 14.3% and 16.1% over the control composition. Flexural strengths reached 6.8 MPa and 7.2 MPa, exceeding the values of the control sample by 23.6% and 26.3%, respectively. Furthermore, water absorption in the C and VC concretes decreased by 41.1% and 53.4%. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis revealed that the modified C and VC composites possess a homogeneous cement matrix structure, characterized by a uniform distribution of hydration reaction products. The resulting variatropic concretes exhibit improved performance characteristics and can be used to manufacture reinforced concrete products with an annular cross-section for industrial and civil infrastructure facilities.
