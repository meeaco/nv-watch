### [Cavity Control of Emission Dynamics from Spin Defects in Hexagonal Boron Nitride](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20751)
- Anuj Kumar Singh et al. (13 authors) · arXiv:2611.20751 · physics.optics (cats: cond-mat.mes-hall, cond-mat.mtrl-sci, physics.optics) · submitted 2026-11-16 · relevance score 3 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Optically addressable negatively charged boron vacancy ($\mathrm{V_B^-}$) defects in hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) are promising for quantum sensing at room temperature. The two-dimensional structure of hBN allows direct access to the local environment, enabling proximal sensing of target materials. However, its broadband emission and low quantum yield hinder efficient light-matter interaction, and the coupling of $\mathrm{V_B^-}$ optical dipoles to confined cavity fields remains poorly understood. In this study, we integrate $\mathrm{V_B^-}$ defects with a tunable plano-concave microcavity, achieving three-dimensional optical confinement. This approach yields photoluminescence (PL) enhancement and cavity-modified optical decay dynamics, providing insights into the orientation of the $\mathrm{V_B^-}$ defect dipole. Time-resolved PL measurements identify a distinct fast decay component, suggesting a complex dipole geometry for the defects. Therefore, a tilted optical dipole model is considered to attribute the fast and slow decay components to in-plane and out-of-plane dipole contributions, respectively. Additionally, optically detected magnetic resonance and excitation-polarisation-dependent PL measurements provide further insights into local symmetry breaking and potential optical dipole configurations. These results highlight the role of optical dipole orientation in $\mathrm{V_B^-}$ defect-cavity coupling.

### [Optimal Quantum Sensing Algorithms for Discrete-Frequency Spectroscopy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21560)
- Yu-Jie Liu et al. (7 authors) · arXiv:2611.21560 · quant-ph (cats: quant-ph) · submitted 2026-11-16 · relevance score 1 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Spectroscopy is a versatile tool that drives scientific discovery and underpins modern technologies. Quantum sensing offers promising opportunities to advance spectroscopy through enhanced sensitivity and spectral and spatial resolution. However, existing approaches largely focus on improving general measurement capabilities, often overlooking the structure of specific sensing tasks and leaving potential performance gains untapped. Here, we develop an algorithmic approach to quantum sensing tailored to various spectroscopic tasks, providing new avenues for improvement. Specifically, we consider general time-varying signals supported on a known set of $N$ candidate frequencies, a setting we call discrete-frequency spectroscopy. We formulate three types of practically motivated sensing tasks---Detection, Search, and Learning---that require progressively more spectral information. For all tasks, we design optimal quantum sensing algorithms achieving a $\sqrt{N}$ speedup over conventional frequency-scanning approaches. Optimal Detection and Search need only a single-qubit sensor under $\pi$-pulse control, whereas optimal Learning provably requires coherent processing with a quantum computer. End-to-end numerical simulations incorporating various experimental imperfections predict up to two-orders-of-magnitude speedup in Detection. As an example, we analyze the feasibility of our Detection and Search algorithms for real-world molecular screening based on state-of-the-art nitrogen-vacancy centers. We also show how quantum-computing-enhanced Learning enables wireless communication in a weak-signal regime where conventional methods fail. Our results establish a practical route toward realizing quantum advantages via designing task-specific sensing algorithms.

### [Folate-Functionalized PAMAM–Nanodiamond Conjugates for Sustained pH-Dependent Cabazitaxel Release and Enhanced Cytotoxicity in Prostate Cancer Cells](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.6c10278)
- Sachin Patil et al. (5 authors) · ACS Omega · created 2026-11-16 · published 2026-11-16 · via Crossref · relevance score 6
- Abstract: Abstract Cabazitaxel (CTX) is an effective chemotherapeutic agent for advanced prostate cancer, but limited aqueous solubility and systemic toxicity motivate alternative delivery strategies. Herein, we developed a folate receptor-targeted nanodiamond-based nanoconjugate (ND-PAMAM-FA-CTX) for selective and controlled drug delivery. Cabazitaxel was first converted to CTX-hemisuccinate and subsequently conjugated to a fourth-generation polyamidoamine (PAMAM) dendrimer along with folic acid (FA), followed by covalent attachment to nanodiamonds (NDs). Comprehensive physicochemical characterization confirmed the successful construction of the nanoconjugate while preserving the structural integrity of the nanodiamond core. The resulting platform exhibited high CTX-loading capacity and a sustained, pH-responsive drug-release profile, with enhanced release under acidic conditions relevant to the tumor microenvironment. The drug-free carrier showed excellent biocompatibility, whereas ND-PAMAM-FA-CTX demonstrated superior anticancer activity against folate receptor (FAR)-overexpressing PC3 prostate cancer cells compared with free CTX and nontargeted controls. Flow cytometry and confocal microscopy revealed significantly enhanced cellular uptake mediated by folate receptor-dependent endocytosis, which was further confirmed through competitive inhibition studies using free folic acid. Moreover, ND-PAMAM-FA-CTX exhibited a low nanomolar IC50 value (∼5 nM) and markedly suppressed PC3 cell migration. Overall, these findings demonstrate that ND-PAMAM-FA-CTX is a promising targeted nanotherapeutic platform that combines selective cellular delivery and controlled drug release with enhanced efficacy, offering potential for future targeted chemotherapy applications.
