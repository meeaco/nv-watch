### [Maximum-Temperature Theorem for a Nanothermometer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24340)
- Taras Plakhotnik · arXiv:2610.24340 · cond-mat.stat-mech (cats: cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.mtrl-sci, physics.class-ph) · submitted 2026-10-29 · relevance score 11 · found by arXiv-OAI
- Abstract: We derive a sharp upper bound on the quasi-steady temperature rise of a sensor of arbitrary shape and known thermometric volume $V_\mathrm{s}$, placed anywhere inside a region enclosed by an isothermal reference surface. The bound also requires the total heat power generated in the region $\dot{Q}_\mathrm{r}$ and a lower bound on its thermal conductivity $κ_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal {V}_{\mathrm{r}}}}$. It is otherwise independent of the heat-source distribution, domain shape, and source--sensor distance. For $\dot Q_\mathrm{r}=50~\mathrm{pW}$, $κ_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm r}}} =0.1~\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}K^{-1}}$, and $V_{\mathrm s}$ equal to the volume of a $60~\mathrm{nm}$-diameter sphere, the theorem permits a maximum temperature rise of approximately $2~\mathrm{mK}$. Neither source localization nor uncertain source--sensor separation can therefore account for reported kelvin-scale intracellular readings under steady-state heat conduction. The result applies to sensors based on nanodiamonds, upconversion nanoparticles, fluorescent polymer beads, and core--shell quantum dots.

### [A Luminescent Organic Triradical as an Optically Addressable Qudit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38762)
- Kueyoung E. Kim et al. (7 authors) · arXiv:2610.38762 · cond-mat.mtrl-sci (cats: cond-mat.mtrl-sci) · submitted 2026-10-28 · relevance score 3 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Luminescent molecular trityl radicals provide options for optically-addressable electron spins and are promising for quantum sensing. Here, we present a luminescent triradical comprising three trityl groups covalently attached to a central phenyl ring. We find two distinct photoluminescence bands at 633 nm and 690 nm with different temporal evolutions, which we attribute to excited quartet and doublet states respectively. The ground state quartet is 1 meV (11.5K) lower in energy than the doublet, resulting in near-equal population of doublets and quartets at room temperature. We demonstrate optically initialized spin polarization, coherent control of the ground state quartet, and optical readout. Potential for optical quantum sensing is shown via optically detected magnetic resonance at room temperature and magneto-photoluminescence up to 3% at 5 mT. This material is a candidate optically addressable molecular qudit, providing opportunities to leverage larger state spaces than qubit systems.

### [Optimized Dynamical Decoupling for Spin Ensembles with Large Inhomogeneities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39693)
- Rajan Paul et al. (7 authors) · arXiv:2610.39693 · quant-ph (cats: quant-ph) · submitted 2026-10-29 · relevance score 2 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Spin ensembles show increasing promise for highly efficient quantum sensing and quantum technologies. However, protecting their coherent properties remains a significant challenge. Dynamical decoupling (DD) typically employs a sequence of fast pulses that aim to reduce the effect of unwanted system-environment interactions. However, the performance of the DD sequences typically suffers in spin ensembles, e.g., due to amplitude and frequency inhomogeneities and control errors. In this work, we compare the performance of an extensive number of DD protocols for coherence protection and quantum sensing in an ensemble of negatively charged NV centers under ambient conditions. We identify the optimal approach that uses rapid adiabatic passage pulses, which are robust to amplitude and frequency inhomogeneity, in combination with the variation of their phases. We experimentally demonstrate an improved signal contrast and an order-of-magnitude increase in memory time with the optimal sequence compared to the widely used XY8 with standard rectangular pulses. We also apply our approach for improved sensing of a weak oscillating field. While our particular demonstration is with NV centers in diamond, the optimized protocol is generally applicable to other spin ensembles and to systems with high amplitude and/or frequency inhomogeneities.

### [High bandwidth heterodyne sensing with bulk NV diamond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39022)
- H. A. M. Taylor et al. (7 authors) · arXiv:2610.39022 · quant-ph (cats: quant-ph) · submitted 2026-10-28 · relevance score 1 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Developing high performance portable sensors is of significant importance for both terrestrial and satellite communications, with quantum sensors offering potential advantages in sensitivity and bandwidth. The nitrogen vacancy center in diamond is a popular quantum sensing platform for its relative simplicity and wide range of acceptable operating conditions, but suffers a limited instantaneous bandwidth due to the narrow linewidth of the spin resonance. In this work, we first present a magnetic quantum sensing scheme utilizing heterodyne mixing with strong optical and microwave driving, achieving MHz-scale instantaneous bandwidth with a sensitivity of $548 \; \text{pT/}\sqrt{\text{Hz}}$ in an active diamond volume of $ 2.5\times 10^{-4} \; \text{mm}^3$ with $2 \times 10^{11}$ NV centers. We then demonstrate expansion of the sensing bandwidth by employing a strong magnetic gradient and many individually modulated microwave control fields, expanding the single resonance bandwidth to 48 MHz with a peak sensitivity of $8.5 \; \text{nT/}\sqrt{\text{Hz}}$.

### [Signatures of Gaussian superconducting fluctuations in nonlocal noise magnetometry](https://doi.org/10.1103/x5ym-1w9j)
- Dror Orgad · Physical review. B./Physical review. B · created 2026-05-21 · published 2026-10-28 · via OpenAlex · relevance score 4
- Abstract: We calculate the two-point magnetic noise spectrum arising from Gaussian superconducting fluctuations, a quantity directly measurable by spin qubit pairs such as nitrogen vacancy centers in diamond. The analysis utilizes the time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau theory, reflecting the direct contribution of fluctuating Cooper pairs to the current correlations and consequent magnetic noise. We treat both two-dimensional systems and wires, considering them in equilibrium and under a uniform electric field. The signal is expected to be strongest in high-temperature superconductors, and we contrast our findings with the predicted signatures of a vortex liquid to offer an additional route to elucidate the nature of fluctuations in these systems.

### [All-fiber quantum current transformer based on nitrogen-vacancy centers](https://doi.org/10.1364/oe.611797)
- Qingtao Guo et al. (11 authors) · Optics Express · created 2026-10-14 · published 2026-10-13 · via OpenAlex · relevance score 1
- Abstract: High-voltage substations impose stringent requirements on current measurement, demanding simultaneously high electrical insulation and stable, high-precision performance. Here we propose and experimentally validate an all-fiber quantum current transformer based on nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond. The transformer employs four optical fibers to realize power delivery and signal transmission between the high-voltage and low-voltage sides, completely eliminating metallic electrical connections. The system achieves a magnetic sensitivity of 4.05 nT/Hz 1/2 , with highly linear current response ( R 2 = 0.998) and a measurement accuracy better than 1%. This all-fiber architecture satisfies the dual constraints of high-voltage insulation and low power consumption while preserving the metrological precision of quantum sensing, providing a viable pathway toward non-invasive precision current monitoring in next-generation smart substations.
