### [Low-field dephasing time of an optically polarized NV$^-$ spin bath beyond the P1 limit in diamond: a cluster-correlation-expansion and exact spin-bath study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29279)
- Chikara Shinei · arXiv:2610.29279 · quant-ph (cats: quant-ph) · submitted 2026-10-21 · relevance score 4
- Abstract: Dense ensembles of nitrogen-vacancy (NV$^-$) centres in nitrogen-rich diamond dephase on the electron spins of the substitutional nitrogen (P1) centres. If the paramagnetic nitrogen is instead converted into NV$^-$ centres, the laser that initializes the sensor also pumps the bath into $m_s$ = 0, a state with no moment along its own axis. Using cluster-correlation-expansion (CCE) calculations, combined with an exact solver for probe-bath exchange and with no adjustable parameter, this paper asks whether such an optically polarized NV$^-$ bath dephases a sensor more slowly than a P1 bath of the same density. The answer is set by the field. In a field along [111], three quarters of the bath centres lie off the field axis, and the moment of their $m_s$ = 0 state along the field grows from 0.04 at 25 G to 0.46 at 300 G and 0.81 at 800 G. At 25 G the polarized bath is therefore nearly silent: at $10^{17}$ cm$^{-3}$ the dephasing time $T_2^*$ of a separately addressed $^{15}$NV$^-$ probe is 112 us, six times the 18.1 us of a P1 bath, and still 2.4 times at 90 % polarization; an ensemble measurement gains a factor of two. At 300 G the advantage over P1 shrinks to 1.4, and at 800 G the polarized bath dephases faster than P1. The field dependence of every bath, P1 included, follows the mean moment of its centres along the field. For the Hahn echo, exchange with on-axis bath centres and bath relaxation ($T_1$ = 6 ms) limit the gain to 1.2 times the P1 value at 25 G. Optical polarization of an NV$^-$ bath at low field therefore offers dephasing times beyond the P1 limit of nitrogen-rich diamond.

### [Strong Thermal Conductivity Suppression in Isotope Purified Single Crystal Diamond with Phonon Scatterings of Color Centers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24988)
- Jinwen Liu et al. (5 authors) · arXiv:2610.24988 · cond-mat.mtrl-sci (cats: cond-mat.mtrl-sci, physics.app-ph) · submitted 2026-10-18 · relevance score 3
- Abstract: The thermal conductivity of natural diamond can be further improved by ~50% by eliminating isotope impurity to obtain pure 12C diamond. However, unlike isolated point defects, how color centers scatter phonons and correspondingly limit the thermal conductivity remain poorly understood. Here, we report the thermal conductivities of two isotope-purified diamond with different Nitrogen-vacancy (NV) color center concentrations, confirmed by photoluminescence (PL) and secondary-ion mass spectrometry (SIMS). Results show ~ 3200 W m-1 K-1 and ~ 2100 W m-1 K-1 for two samples of low and high NV concentrations, respectively. Modified Callaway model analyses show that first-principles-derived scattering parameters can reproduce room-temperature thermal conductivity of the high NV concentration sample under a vacancy concentration assumption (~30% of the total Nitrogen concentration), but fail to reproduce its temperature dependence. Compared to PL and SIMS results, which show ~7.1-fold NV concentration and ~92-fold difference in total Nitrogen concentration between two samples, suggesting the assumed 30% vacancy fraction may be greatly overestimated. Taken together, these results suggest that NV-color-center defects may scatter phonons more strongly than models treating Nitrogen and vacancy as independent point defects, providing new insights into color-center-induced phonon-scattering mechanisms.

### [Low-Field J-Coupling Spectroscopy Measured with a Diamond Nitrogen Vacancy Spectrometer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24815)
- Stephen J. DeVience, John W. Blanchard, Camille G. McDonnell, Ronald L. Walsworth · arXiv:2610.24815 · physics.chem-ph (cats: physics.chem-ph, physics.app-ph, physics.atom-ph, quant-ph) · submitted 2026-10-17 · relevance score 0
- Abstract: Nitrogen vacancy centers in diamond (NVs) are point defects well suited to quantum sensing of magnetic fields. NV spin states can be initialized and read out optically with high spatial resolution, enabling detection of pL-scale volumes of nuclear spins. Using Coherently Averaged Synchronized Readout (CASR), standard NMR spectroscopy techniques such as free decay, spin echo, and PGSE have been performed with Hz-scale spectral resolution using NV-diamond sensors. However, using NV detection for conventional high-resolution NMR at multi-tesla fields is challenging due to the need to manipulate NV electronic spins at resonance frequencies 100 GHz or higher. Low-field J-coupling spectroscopy provides an alternative path to chemical resolution in a regime more easily accessible to NVs. Here, we demonstrate homonuclear and heteronuclear J-coupling spectroscopy using an NV-NMR spectrometer based on permanent magnets operating at $\sim$890 kHz proton Larmor frequency (bias magnetic field $\sim$21 mT). This field strength enables chemical characterization based on J-coupling using sequences such as Synchronized Echo (SyncE) and Spin-Echo Double Resonance (SEDOR), which do not require the strong chemical shift dispersion and pristine field homogeneity of high-field NMR. The frequency range is easily detected by NVs using standard XY dynamical decoupling sequences and is well-suited for applying Overhauser DNP, which can boost proton signal by two orders of magnitude. We acquire initial NV-NMR measurements of T$_1$, T$_2$, and Overhauser DNP in glycerol before advancing to SEDOR spectroscopy of methanol-$^{13}$C and a methanol-$^{13}$C/acetonitrile-2-$^{13}$C mixture at sample volumes $\sim$50 pL, in which we demonstrate measurement of $^1J_\text{CH}$ couplings and resolution between species. We also acquire a homonuclear J-coupling spectrum of a natural-abundance propylene carbonate sample.

### [Two-qubit cryo-CMOS control of a spin register in diamond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26413)
- H. B. van Ommen et al. (9 authors) · arXiv:2610.26413 · quant-ph (cats: quant-ph) · submitted 2026-10-19 · relevance score 0
- Abstract: Utility-scale quantum technologies will not only require a well-performing quantum platform, but also classical control hardware that is scalable, efficient, and meets the design requirements set by the quantum system of interest. One approach is the application of silicon CMOS technology, a highly scalable and mature technology, to design application-specific integrated circuits (ASIC) that are optimized for the quantum system. In this work, we present two-qubit control of electron and nuclear spins in NV centres in diamond, using a cryogenic CMOS qubit controller integrated with a diamond quantum device in a cryostat. Using Gate Set Tomography (GST) we characterize the performance of the single-qubit gates on both electron- and carbon nuclear spins, and analyze the limiting noise processes. Our demonstration of cryo-CMOS-driven two-qubit gates is a key step towards highly-integrated quantum devices based on colour centres.

### [Laser-enhanced quantum sensing boosts sensitivity and dynamic range](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78386-z)
- Florian Schall et al. (11 authors) · Nature Communications · created 2026-10-22 · published 2026-10-22 · via Crossref · relevance score 4
- Abstract: Abstract While magnetometers based on nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond have emerged as the most important solid-state quantum sensors, ensembles are typically limited to a few percent optical contrast. Here, we demonstrate a laser threshold magnetometry-based NV system that achieves 100% optical contrast in combination with strong output signals up to 50 mW. By integrating the NV centers into a laser cavity, we show magnetic-field-dependent shifts of the laser threshold, allowing the laser system to be entirely switched off with the NV centers’ magnetic resonance. This provides a perspective for highly sensitive magnetometry operated outside magnetically-shielded rooms for applications including magnetoencephalography, magnetic navigation, and magnetic anomaly detection.

### [Improved state readout in NV centers using regression models and rabi driving](https://doi.org/10.1088/1402-4896/aeb758)
- Fritz Joseph Joseph Haltenberger, Manpreet Singh Jattana · Physica Scripta · created 2026-10-21 · published 2026-10-21 · via Crossref · relevance score 3
- Abstract: Abstract Readout of state populations in nitrogen-vacancy centers from fluorescence measurements at roomtemperature is routinely achieved via contrast-based calibration. The fidelities achieved by this conventional approach are limited by reducing the dynamical fluorescence behaviour of the NV center to a scalar value, and calculating the population of each possible state independently. To address these limitations, we use regression models trained on experimental data to map the fluorescence signals onto ideal simulated populations. Additionally, we enhance the informational content of the fluorescence signals by performing measurements during induced Rabi oscillations. Our results demonstrate that including these dynamical signals significantly reduces state readout errors across multiple tested models. Notably, linear ridge regression performs nearly on par with a non-linear kernel-based model, showing that simple models already capture the relevant mapping between the enhanced fluorescence signals and the underlying state populations. This data-driven approach provides a robust alternative that achieves higher fidelities than conventional calibration in our setting, paving the way for high-fidelity state readout in solid-state quantum registers.
