### [Nitrogen-Vacancy Nuclear Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy of $^{15}$N Spins in Potassium Nitrite Solution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33360)
- Sehrish Iqbal et al. (6 authors) · arXiv:2611.33360 · quant-ph (cats: cond-mat.mes-hall, quant-ph) · submitted 2026-11-24 · relevance score 5 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Nitrogen is essential in agricultural fertilizers, yet its nanoscale interfacial dynamics remain largely unexplored. Although quadrupolar broadening complicates $^{14}$N nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) detection, $^{15}$N (I=1/2) provides well-defined nuclear-spin signatures but remains challenging to detect at the nanoscale due to its low natural abundance and small gyromagnetic ratio. Here, we use a microfluidic quantum sensing platform based on shallow nitrogen vacancy (NV) centers in diamond to detect $^{15}$N NMR from KNO$_{3}$ solution at room temperature and in a low magnetic field regime. Within femtoliter-scale sensing volumes, we measure a magnetic-field variance of up to ~442 nT$^{2}$, ~43 times greater than the expected statistical polarization. The $^{15}$N spin correlation decays within ~500 $\mu\mathrm{s}$, mainly limited by the NV spin lattice relaxation time and not by the molecular diffusion of the KNO$_{3}$ molecules. This is consistent with the formation of a KNO$_{3}$ adsorption layer on the diamond surface, supported by surface characterization.

### [Phonon-Aware Electron-to-Nuclear Spin Quantum Memory in NV Centers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33936)
- Manjith Baby Sarojam Chellam · arXiv:2611.33936 · quant-ph (cats: quant-ph) · submitted 2026-11-25 · relevance score 5 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond are a promising solid-state platform for quantum memory: the electron spin can be optically initialized and read out, while nearby nuclear spins have long coherence times and can serve as storage qubits. In this work, we develop a phonon-aware open quantum system model for electron-to-nuclear spin state transfer in a single NV center. The model includes the NV electron spin, a coupled 13C nuclear spin, the hyperfine interaction, an effective microwave/radio-frequency-assisted SWAP interaction for writing and reading, a single effective phonon mode with longitudinal spin-phonon coupling, and Lindblad relaxation and dephasing channels. The dynamics are simulated with the Lindblad master equation in QuTiP, using the secular approximation and a hyperfine-resonant drive in the rotating frame. Without spin-phonon coupling and electron decoherence, the write-store-read protocol recovers the stored state with a fidelity above 0.9999. With the default decoherence rates, increasing the spin-phonon coupling from 0 to 0.06 MHz lowers the worst-case retrieval fidelity from 0.9994 to 0.9884, and the fidelity oscillates with the phonon-mode period because electron-phonon entanglement generated during the write step is partially undone during the read step. With a phenomenological temperature-dependent rate model, the fidelity decreases monotonically from 0.9997 at 1 K to 0.9938 at 300 K, while storage-time losses follow the assumed nuclear-spin decoherence rates. The storage-time plots, temperature sweep, and parameter maps identify operating regimes for NV-center quantum memory, and the accompanying open-source code provides a reproducible framework for studying phonon-limited quantum memory.

### [Infrared-absorption magnetometry in a diamond nanocavity: demonstration and limits at the nanoscale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33481)
- Nicholas J. Sorensen et al. (10 authors) · arXiv:2611.33481 · quant-ph (cats: cond-mat.mes-hall, physics.app-ph, physics.optics, quant-ph) · submitted 2026-11-24 · relevance score 4 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Diamond quantum sensors promise both high spatial resolution and high sensitivity; however, conventional photoluminescence-based spin readout can be difficult to integrate efficiently. Infrared absorption by nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centres in diamond provides a promising alternative, although the sensitivity of compact absorption-based sensors is limited by the short optical interaction length between the probe field and the NV ensemble. To overcome this limitation, we demonstrate, for the first time, singlet-absorption magnetometry using spin-dense diamond nanocavities. By probing cavity modes near the 1042 nm NV singlet transition, we observe spin-dependent cavity transmission and perform cavity-enhanced magnetic field sensing. We further develop and experimentally validate a model that accurately predicts the spin-dependent cavity response, describes the optimal operating conditions, and identifies the practical limitations of different optically detected magnetic resonance modalities. Together, these results establish cavity-enhanced singlet absorption as a scalable readout mechanism for integrated diamond quantum sensors and provide a practical framework for engineering next-generation nanophotonic diamond quantum magnetometers.

### [Dimensional crossover to a slow disordered spin bath below the mean spin spacing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33699)

- Abstract: How thin must a layer of spins be to act as a two-dimensional bath? For nitrogen spins around an NV$^-$ centre in diamond, a cluster expansion with flip-flopping outer spins gives a crossover near the mean spin spacing. Thin layers follow two-dimensional scaling with a prefactor six to seven times that of bulk. ### [On the performance of nanoscale detection methods in NV-center spectroscopy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34321)
- Koushik Paul, Pol Alsina-Bol\'ivar, Jorge Casanova · arXiv:2611.34321 · quant-ph (cats: quant-ph) · submitted 2026-11-25 · relevance score 1 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) in subfemtoliter volumes operates in a regime where statistical fluctuations of the nuclear magnetization can exceed its thermal mean. Solid-state quantum sensors such as nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond are sensitive enough to detect NMR signals from such small volumes, which has motivated several detection protocols, based either on correlating the statistical polarization or on coherently detecting hyperpolarized samples. Here we bring Qdyne, CS, MCS, and CASR into a single framework for nanoscale samples. Specifically, we derive a unified analytical expression for the SNR and spin-number sensitivity applicable to all four protocols under consistent experimental conditions. Numerical simulations of the full measurement sequences confirm this expression over the whole range of parameters explored. This formulation enables a quantitative comparison of their performance while accounting for the distinct measurement sequences, acquisition times, and other related parameters. Using the SNR and the spin-number sensitivity as figures of merit, we identify which protocol performs best in each experimental regime, thereby providing a practical guide for choosing and tuning the most suitable method for a given experiment.

### [Mode-resolved spin-wave spectroscopy by a single paramagnetic center](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34484)
- Cl\'ement Fourniols et al. (7 authors) · arXiv:2611.34484 · cond-mat.mes-hall (cats: cond-mat.mes-hall) · submitted 2026-11-25 · relevance score 0 · found by arXiv-RSS+arXiv-OAI+arXiv-API
- Abstract: We demonstrate mode-resolved ferromagnetic resonance (FMR) spectroscopy using optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) of a single nitrogen-vacancy (NV) center in (111)-oriented diamond. A two-tone drive couples the magnetization dynamics to the NV spin resonance via the magnon Kerr effect. We show that the NV can detect the static stray field produced by this effect down to precession cone angles $\theta \approx \SI{1}{\degree}$, providing a generic scheme to measure the linear FMR spectrum with the spatial resolution of an atomic-size probe. We validate the approach on a normally magnetized yttrium iron garnet (YIG) microdisk, resolving its intrinsic quantized spin-wave modes. Scanning the probe across the surface, we map the spatial profile of the modes and find quantitative agreement with analytical models.

### [A deep-learning-boosted framework for quantum sensing with nitrogen-vacancy centers in diamond](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2612349123)
- Changyu Yao et al. (16 authors) · Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences · created 2026-11-25 · published 2026-11-25 · via Crossref · relevance score 11
- Abstract: Nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond are a versatile quantum sensing platform for high sensitivity measurements of magnetic fields, temperature, and strain with nanoscale spatial resolution. A common bottleneck is the analysis of optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) spectra, where target quantities are encoded in resonance features. Conventional nonlinear fitting is often computationally expensive, sensitive to initialization, and prone to failure at low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). Here we introduce a robust, efficient machine learning (ML) framework for real-time ODMR analysis based on a one-dimensional convolutional neural network (1D-CNN). The model performs direct parameter inference without initial guesses or iterative optimization, and is naturally parallelizable on graphics processing units (GPU) for high-throughput processing. We validate the approach on both synthetic and experimental datasets, showing improved throughput, accuracy, and robustness than standard nonlinear fitting, with the largest gains in the low-SNR regime. We further validate our methods in two representative sensing applications: diagnosing intracellular temperature changes using nanodiamond probes and widefield magnetic imaging of superconducting vortices in a high-temperature superconductor. This deep-learning inference framework enables fast and reliable extraction of physical parameters from complex ODMR data and provides a scalable route to real-time quantum sensing and imaging.
